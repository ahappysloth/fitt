# Fitt form-guide foundation

The first 3D prototype is integrated into **Knee Push-up** in both the workout and exercise detail. The admin and standalone studio additionally preview Bodyweight Squat and Pull-up using the same viewer and body models. Those two prototypes are not enabled in ordinary workout or exercise-detail guides yet; all existing illustrated guides and thumbnails remain available. A dedicated review page is generated by `npm run build:form-preview` at `preview/fitt-form-studio.html`.

## Recommended direction

Use one canonical human rig, selectable surface models, and one shared viewer, with an independent movement definition for each exercise. Keep satin-gray materials, a quiet studio background, and electric-lime emphasis consistent across the library. Preset cameras give beginners useful views; free orbit is available when they want to explore.

The male model is the default, with a broader chest, defined shoulder and arm contours, and a leaner waist. The female option has a slender adult silhouette with softer shoulders, a tapered waist, rounded hips and glutes, and a lifted chest curve. Both use authored muscle targets and bounded surface sculpting on the same base topology, with a shared satin-gray finish and soft fill lighting. The guide's model picker and Settings share a local preference, also included in backups. Earlier saves default to male without changing training data. Model switching selects cached surface geometry and normals while preserving the shared skeleton, current pose, playback, camera, muscle focus, and WebGL context. The standalone study lets reviewers switch models independently of app data.

The study also includes a standing model inspector below the exercise viewer. It shares the model selection and initially shows the unposed mesh without muscle highlighting. Free orbit, pan, close-up zoom, front/side/back presets, and head/feet/full-body framing support anatomy review. Both studio viewers map left-drag to rotation, middle-drag to panning, and the scroll wheel to zoom. One-finger rotation, two-finger pan/pinch, keyboard controls, and the inspector's visible movement buttons support other input methods. Its separate WebGL context initializes near the viewport and renders only after a camera, model, highlight, or size change.

The admin and standalone studios offer a shared **Muscle preview** panel. Its independent toggles cover chest, shoulders, biceps, triceps, forearms, upper back, lower back, core, quads, glutes, hamstrings, and calves. Any combination is rendered by the same canonical surface masks in both the standing and animated models. The panel supports Select all, Clear, and disabling preview while remembering the selection. Preview state stays local to the studio and preserves the selected body, camera, playback, and position in the rep. Ordinary workout guides retain their exercise-specific controls; Knee Push-up's All option continues to mean chest, triceps, and shoulders.

Highlight boundaries use a restrained feather after the selected mask weights are interpolated in the fragment shader. This reduces the broad glow while preserving the existing region contours and the model's lighting. A scalar weight attribute follows the same skinned vertices through body switches and motion; it does not change geometry or require an extra rendering pass.

A complete skeleton and individually modeled muscles are a **content upgrade**, not a reason to replace the viewer. The prototype already has a real bone rig and weighted mesh. Its muscle highlights are approximate surface masks. The next anatomy asset should supply named muscle groups and optional bone meshes, all bound to the same canonical rig, or include a deliberate retargeting step. A detailed medical-looking asset still needs accurate movement clips and deformation work; more polygons alone will not fix form.

## Separation of responsibilities

| Layer                                                                          | Responsibility                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `body-data.json`, `male-body-data.json`, `female-body-data.json`, `athlete.ts` | Local surfaces, shared skeleton/weights, appearance, and muscle-region selection. Applies a pose without knowing the exercise. |
| `contracts.ts`                                                                 | Pose transforms, contact landmarks, movement timing, phases, keyframes, instructions, and selectable regions.                  |
| `muscle-regions.ts`, `MusclePreviewControls.tsx`                               | Shared canonical surface masks and the studio's independent muscle-group toggles.                                              |
| `knee-pushup.ts`                                                               | Contact-aware knee push-up motion and coaching cues. No camera, UI, or rendering resources.                                    |
| `scene.ts`                                                                     | Three.js lighting, mat, orbit/preset cameras, zoom, visibility-aware rendering, and resource ownership.                        |
| `KneePushupGuide.tsx`                                                          | Reusable `FormGuide` playback/scrubbing/region controls plus the small Knee Push-up adapter.                                   |

The source rig divides each upper/lower arm and leg into multiple bones. The motion solves each anatomical segment once and distributes its world transform across those subdivisions. Wrists, metacarpals, and fingers share a stable hand frame. Knees remain supported; the top stops short of a locked elbow. The lowered pose brings the forearms close to vertical and includes a brief pause before pressing up.

The calves and relaxed feet follow the torso's angular change around the planted knees, preserving the knee bend and the ankle angle through the rep. This demonstrates one raised-feet variation of the knee push-up.

## Rendering and accessibility

- The guide starts paused. Explicit playback is allowed even with reduced-motion preferences; camera presets do not animate.
- Drag to orbit, choose front/side/three-quarter views, or use rotation/zoom buttons. The canvas also supports arrow keys, +/−, and Home.
- The native range input supports keyboard scrubbing and announces the current phase. Changing keyframes or scrubbing pauses playback.
- Playback updates the timeline directly from the same animation frame as the pose. React updates coaching at phase/cue boundaries; it does not control the range value. Pointer scrubbing pauses immediately, and seeking cancels a queued playback frame so it cannot overwrite the user's position.
- A paused scene renders only when something changes. Animation stops rendering outside the viewport, behind a modal, or in a hidden tab. Pixel density is capped to limit mobile GPU work.
- All mesh data, code, fonts, and styles are bundled locally. React lazy-loads the 3D module; the PWA caches its production chunks for offline use.
- If WebGL cannot initialize or its context is lost, the app retains the existing illustrated guide; the standalone study retains written instructions. Partial initialization failures and normal unmount both release canvases, observers, handlers, geometry, materials, skeleton textures, shadow maps, and the renderer.

## Acceptance before scaling the library

Review this model's silhouette, material, movement, and camera framing first. Then settle the anatomy asset and export/rig contract. For each new movement, review setup, middle, bottom, and return from front and side; check contact, range, alignment, and interference with equipment. A qualified exercise coach should approve the final instructional movement before release.

Unit tests verify constant limb lengths, calf/foot follow-through, fixed contact anchors, a seamless loop, stable skinned palms, knee-surface clearance, finite deformation for both models, and bottom forearm alignment. Surface checks guard both heads against severe facial stretching, preserve exact palm/knee contacts, and keep the female refinement bounded with unchanged lower legs. Muscle checks cover all 12 groups, front/back separation, central abdominal and glute coverage, and combined highlights without changes to pose or shape. They also cover shape switching and preference migration/validation. Browser checks cover model selection/persistence without changing workouts, shared preview selections, playback, scrubbing, camera controls, reduced-motion defaults, local/embedded previews, fallback, initialization cleanup, repeated dialog use, mobile fit, and the existing offline workflow.

The approved knee push-up retains its existing segment transforms. New full-body motions can instead supply exact bone-name transforms, which take precedence in `athlete.applyPose`. `full-body-rig.ts` provides subtree inheritance, anatomical limb frames and constant-length two-bone IK. Whole facial subtrees follow the head together; hip/leg, torso, shoulder girdle and hand/finger overrides can be authored independently without changing mesh assets or materials.

Overhead pull-up arms retain the anatomical front (Z) when framing the upper arm. The forearm carries that upper-arm orientation and bends toward its solved wrist target, preserving the elbow's natural fold. Reusing the squat's lateral (X) axis for elevated arms reverses the front/back frame, introducing a half-turn of axial twist and collapsing the weighted shoulder surface. These overhead settings are explicit; other motions retain their existing frames.

`squat.ts` and `pull-up.ts` each provide a `GuideMotion`. Optional `stage` metadata supplies full-cycle camera framing and a pull-up bar specification. `scene.ts` owns the bar and support geometry, lighting, disposal and cameras. Muscle focus uses each motion's regions; All no longer implies knee push-up muscles in the shared scene. Selecting another studio exercise remounts the keyed guide paused at its setup pose, while retaining the studio's body selection and independent muscle-preview state.

The new guides are review prototypes. The squat demonstrates moderate controlled depth with fixed soles; the shared placement leaves the female sole about 9 mm above the male sole because the surfaces differ. The pull-up uses a fixed authored phalange grip rather than a contact simulator. Shoulder skin uses the shared linear skinning rather than authored pose-corrective shapes. These limitations must be resolved or explicitly accepted before enabling these guides in training. This work does not establish coaching approval.

Motion tests sample the cycle for limb lengths, continuity, seam matching, finite skinned surfaces, fixed support/grip vertices and ground clearance on both models. Pull-up regressions also check upper-arm facing and the lower tail of actual shoulder triangle areas, since correct joint endpoints alone did not detect the collapsed surface. Browser coverage switches exercises, phases, camera, body and muscle selections, verifies playback/scrubbing, checks old-canvas removal and retains the existing knee and offline tests.
